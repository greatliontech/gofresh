# An interface invoke's enumerated targets are not narrowed by the operand

The computed-call narrowing takes a call's targets from the functions
its operand can hold, but an interface method invoke keeps the
enumeration's whole projection: every method of the name on every
type the mask carries. The chunk-125 sweep over the pinned cerebro
tree priced the invoke form at 169 subjects refusing at the startup
walk on archive/zip.Read and 36 on the property harness reached
through (*encoding/json.MarshalerError).Error — standard methods no
user operand holds.

The collapse: the same collector, through its MakeInterface arm,
yields the concrete types an interface operand can hold; the invoke's
targets are those types' methods of the invoked name (resolved through
the program's method sets), within the enumeration's own set, with an
operand the walk cannot close, a closed operand naming no concrete
type, or a method the concrete type promotes from an unscanned body
keeping the projection whole. The subject walk's scan then resolves
such an invoke by the operand alone exactly as it resolves a computed
call.

Lands: with docs/issues/dispatch-admissions-one-predicate.md's
collapse — the one predicate over site, operand, and targets is where
both forms meet (the field report is the chunk-125 sweep above).
